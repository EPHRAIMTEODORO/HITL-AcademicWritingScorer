MEASURE

Observable evidence

Lexical, syntactic, cohesion, and grammar-related measurements from Python.

INTERPRET

Rubric judgments

Local Llama 3 assesses seven traits, then generates a placement recommendation.

DECIDE

Human responsibility

The rater reads the essay and evidence, then accepts or overrides the recommendation.

What “hybrid” means here

The desktop brings rule-based evidence and LLM judgments together for review. Its Model Score is llm_recommended_score. The manual 0–100 AES score is separate; there is no learned fusion or arithmetic average producing the desktop grade.

Scope: the Electron desktop application, its Python backend, and the shared extractor it imports. Separate batch, Colab, research-fusion, and React prototype workflows are outside this guide.

02 / THE WORKFLOW

An essay becomes evidence.
A rater makes it a decision.

The interface has two stages: process a raw workbook, then load a completed workbook for essay-by-essay review. Loading a completed workbook does not call the model again.

  1. Upload & map the workbook

    Choose an .xlsx file and identify the ID, topic, and essay columns. Defaults are Student ID, Topic, and Essay. The packaged sample uses id; the Sample action maps it and processes one essay.

  2. Normalize & measure

    The processor reads the selected sheet, ignores blank headers and wholly empty rows, and cleans known spacing artifacts. It preserves the student's grammar. Python extracts the linguistic features and counts visible paragraphs.

  3. Ask the local rubric assessor

    The model receives a system instruction, the six-level rubric, full Level 4/5/6 anchors, and the target essay with its ID, topic, and paragraph count. MTLD, AWL, grammar density, and the manual AES score are calculated alongside it and are not included in the model prompt.

  4. Write the completed workbook

    Each essay receives measured features, rubric scores, and a justification. After the entire workbook succeeds, the application writes Completed_Scores. Every new human decision is Pending, with no final human grade.

  5. Review, accept or override, and save

    Load the completed workbook, inspect the full essay and supporting evidence, and select a whole-number grade from 1 to 6. Saving updates the records and rewrites the output workbook with a derived placement and timestamp.

Input detail that matters: preserve blank lines between paragraphs inside each essay cell. Paragraph quantity comes from visible blank-line-separated blocks, rather than a discourse-segmentation model.

Implementation: hitl_processor.py → hybrid_llm_aes.evaluate_row() → app_backend.update_job_decision(). Source register ↗

What the code can count.

The separate manual AES score: formula and worked example

Only four of the measurements enter aes_score. The normalization bounds and weights are hand-chosen.

Manual 0–100 heuristic
ComponentNormalization intervalDirection receiving creditWeight
MTLD20–100Higher0.25
AWL ratio0–0.20Higher0.30
Mean Zipf frequency2–6Lower0.20
Grammar matches / 1000–20Lower0.25
N(x) = clip((x − minimum) / (maximum − minimum), 0, 1)
For lower-is-better components: use 1 − N(x)

aes_score = 100 × Σ(weight × normalized value)
                  / Σ(weights of available components)

For MTLD = 60, AWL = 0.10, Zipf = 4, and grammar density = 4, the normalized components are 0.50, 0.50, 0.50, and 0.80. The score is 57.5 / 100. Missing grammar is excluded and the available weights are renormalized. This value does not become the desktop’s Model Score or final human grade.

Two kinds of grammar evidence: LanguageTool counts surface rule matches. The LLM judges their consequences for meaning. The prompt explicitly tells the model not to penalize grammar twice.

The six levels encoded in the prompt

The following is a reading aid. The exact wording is in the prompt library. These are repository descriptors, not an independent verification of institutional placement policy.

6

Clearly organized & fully developed

Accurate cohesive devices; 4+ paragraphs; developed concrete support and some abstract elaboration; strong grammar control with infrequent errors; easily understood by unfamiliar native readers.

5

Clear organization & concrete support

Some accurate cohesive devices; 4 paragraphs; concrete supporting detail; moderate vocabulary and grammar control; errors do not block meaning; understandable with some effort.

4

Partially organized & generally understandable

Limited or inconsistent cohesion; 2–3 paragraphs; personal and sometimes concrete detail; errors may distort meaning, though the essay is generally understandable.

3

Weak organization & limited development

Listed or loosely connected ideas; 1–2 paragraphs, or underdeveloped paragraphs; limited, repetitive, or general support; frequent errors may distort meaning; understandable only with effort.

2

Minimal organization & little relevant support

Very limited development; ideas difficult to follow; frequent errors often block meaning; difficult for unfamiliar native readers to understand.

1

Very limited response & mostly unclear meaning

Fragmentary, memorized, mostly off-topic, or very limited writing; little or no development; insufficient control for Level 2.

Paragraph descriptors guide the model; there are no hard-coded paragraph gates that force a grade. The prompt requires clear Level 4 evidence for grades ≥4, and clearly adequate organization, development, support, and comprehensibility for grades ≥5.

Source: HITL/llm_rubric_prompt.py. The review panel displays five trait aliases; abstract elaboration and grammar meaning impact remain in the output columns.

What the response handler enforces

The parser accepts JSON objects and can recover an object surrounded by extra text. It then rounds scores with floor(value + 0.5) and clamps them to 1–6. Missing or ordinary nonnumeric score values fall back to 1. It does not enforce a complete required-key/type schema, so valid JSON can still produce an incomplete or misleading assessment.

There are two retry budgets: up to two retries for invalid JSON and up to two recoveries for supported runtime interruptions. All retries share the original deadline. Missing-model or missing-endpoint errors such as HTTP 404 fail directly.

Reproducibility boundary: normal essay requests do not explicitly set a seed, context length, or output-token limit. Temperature zero does not guarantee identical output across machines or runtime/model versions. The full rubric, anchors, and essay must fit the effective model context.

These are application display heuristics. They are not calibrated confidence estimates, placement cutoffs, or another scoring model.

The unweighted average of available ranks becomes low (<1.7), developing (1.7 to <2.6), or strong (≥2.6). The model grade is banded as low (1–3), developing (4), or strong (5–6). A mismatch appears only when the bands differ by at least two ranks: low versus strong.

10 / OFFLINE DESKTOP RUNTIME

Local by design.
Prepared before use.

In the default desktop configuration, both the API and model inference run on the same computer. The renderer has no external scripts or fonts. Offline scoring requires the model and language resources to be installed or bundled beforehand.

Startup & process ownership

Electron prefers API port 8765, falls back to an available port, and selects a free private Ollama port. It provisions bundled models, starts the frozen Python sidecar when available, and waits for /health before opening the dashboard.

The backend owns its managed Ollama instance and can recover supported interruptions. An explicit HITL_OLLAMA_URL selects an external endpoint that the ownership logic does not stop or restart.

Availability is different from assessment validity

/health establishes that the backend responds. Preflight checks local resources, packages, Java, model availability, writable paths, and disk space. Test Scoring runs a tiny generation probe with a 120-second timeout and a 32-token output budget; it does not evaluate essay-scoring accuracy.

11 / INTERPRETATION & LIMITS

What an informed reader
should keep in view.

The app makes linguistic evidence and model judgments inspectable. Their presence alone does not establish placement validity, fairness, or rater reliability.

Construct coverage

Length, rarity, connective counts, and parser features are proxies. They cannot by themselves establish argument quality, rhetorical appropriateness, or communicative success.

Prompt & anchor dependence

Only Levels 4–6 have calibration essays. A short topic label supplies less task information than the full writing prompt. Anchor coverage and prompt wording can influence judgments.

Model reliability

Context limits, generated inconsistencies, bias, and essay-embedded instructions need evaluation. A fluent justification is generated assessment text, not a guarantee of correctness or a view of internal reasoning.

Validation needed

This guide makes no accuracy claim for the current desktop release. Establish human–human agreement, evaluate independent prompt-diverse data, and test repeated scoring and subgroup behavior before making stronger claims.

Human review records

The app stores the latest decision in the workbook. This path has no authenticated rater identity or append-only revision history. Human overrides do not trigger automatic learning.

Data & execution boundaries

Default scoring is local, but the API has no application authentication and workbook files are not encrypted by this implementation. A late scoring failure can prevent output creation for the entire workbook.

THE ROLE OF THE HUMAN

Use the measurements to ask better questions.
Use the rubric to inspect the recommendation.
Use professional judgment to decide.
